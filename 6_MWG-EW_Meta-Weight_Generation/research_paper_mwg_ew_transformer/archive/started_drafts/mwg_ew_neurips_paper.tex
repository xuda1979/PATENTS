\begin{abstract}
We introduce \textbf{MWG-EW} (Meta-Weight Generation for Ephemeral Weights), a new neural network architecture in which a substantial portion of a Transformer's projection weights are not stored as static tensors at all, but are \emph{generated on the fly} by a small, learned \emph{meta-weight generator} conditioned on the current token, layer index, and a low-dimensional learned context. The generated weights live as \emph{ephemeral descriptor tiles} inside on-chip SRAM, are immediately consumed by an associative low-rank kernel $Y=(XU)V$, and are released before the next tile is produced --- they are never written back to off-chip memory. This redefines the operand lifecycle of a Transformer FFN block and turns external-memory traffic, not arithmetic intensity, into the design knob.

We give: (i) a formal architectural description of the meta-generator and its conditional low-rank head; (ii) an end-to-end differentiable training procedure with a stability-regularized energy-aware loss; (iii) tight bandwidth, latency and KV-cache capacity bounds; and (iv) approximation guarantees that connect tail singular value energy to gated-FFN output error. Empirically, on \textbf{Ascend\,910B2 (8 NPUs, torch\_npu 2.6.0)}, MWG-EW achieves a measured \textbf{2.39$\times$} decode latency speedup at $r{=}128$, a measured \textbf{8.72$\times$} training-step speedup at $r{=}32$ (batch~4, seq~512), \textbf{99.6$\times$} reduction in weight traffic, \textbf{24.89$\times$} reduction in distributed gradient bytes (336.0~MiB $\to$ 13.5~MiB), and frees \textbf{322.5~MiB} of HBM that we re-purpose into \textbf{+430} additional KV tokens under a fixed memory budget. End-to-end perplexity on WikiText-103 with a Qwen-1.5B backbone degrades by only \textbf{+0.31} at the rank that delivers $24.9\times$ traffic reduction. To our knowledge this is the first architecture that shows simultaneously (a) wall-clock speedup, (b) order-of-magnitude bandwidth savings, (c) reduced gradient communication, and (d) increased usable KV cache --- on the same model, on real silicon, with a single mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention} and their large language descendants are dominated, both at training and at inference, by the cost of \emph{moving} feed-forward weights between HBM and on-chip SRAM. On modern accelerators the gap between peak FLOPs and sustainable HBM bandwidth has widened to more than $200{:}1$, so a typical FFN forward pass spends the overwhelming majority of its wall-clock time waiting for weight bytes, not multiplying them. We refer to this regime as the \emph{bandwidth wall}.

Existing remedies attack symptoms rather than the cause. Quantization, weight pruning and static low-rank decomposition shrink the bytes per weight, but the weights are still \emph{materialized in DRAM} and \emph{streamed every token}. Hypernetworks \cite{ha2017hypernetworks} generate weights from context but are typically used at the granularity of an entire matrix per task and are not designed to interact with on-chip operand lifecycles. Mixture-of-Experts shifts the problem to expert routing and aggravates communication.

\textbf{Our position.} Bandwidth, on-chip residency, and learnability should be co-designed inside a single architectural primitive. We propose to replace each gated FFN projection by a \emph{conditional meta-weight generator} that emits two thin matrices $U_r,V_r$ directly into SRAM. The downstream kernel uses the algebraic identity $XW \approx (XU_r)V_r$ to consume the descriptors immediately, and the lifecycle controller guarantees the descriptors are overwritten before the next tile arrives. Crucially, the meta-generator is itself a (small) neural network whose parameters are learned end-to-end with the rest of the model.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Architecture.} A new Transformer block in which FFN projections are produced by a learned conditional generator rather than read from DRAM (Sec.~\ref{sec:arch}). The generator amortizes across a token group of size $g$ and an expert basis-bank of size $K$, exposing two interpretable knobs: rank $r$ and reuse $g$.
\item \textbf{Theory.} Closed-form bandwidth, latency and KV-cache bounds (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}); a Lipschitz-style end-to-end approximation theorem for gated FFNs (Thm.~\ref{thm:gated_ffn}); and a sufficient condition $\Gamma(r){<}1$ for unconditional latency improvement.
\item \textbf{Numerical evidence on real silicon.} Measured Ascend\,910B2 results showing $2.39\times$ decode and up to $8.72\times$ training speedups, $99.6\times$ traffic reduction, $24.89\times$ gradient reduction and $+430$ additional KV tokens (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}). Quality is preserved on WikiText-103 and on a Qwen-1.5B SVD-energy benchmark.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} Open scripts \texttt{run\_real\_benchmark.py}, \texttt{profile\_descriptor\_lifecycle.py}, and \texttt{svd\_pretrained\_analysis.py}; all numbers carry a backend tag (\textsc{measured} vs.\ \textsc{predicted}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hypernetworks and dynamic weights.} Hypernetworks \cite{ha2017hypernetworks} generate the weights of a target network from a learned context. Subsequent work explores task-conditional generation, modulation and FiLM-style conditioning. MWG-EW is, in spirit, a hypernetwork; the difference is that the generator's output is treated as an \emph{ephemeral hardware operand} rather than a persistent parameter, and is sized exactly to fit on-chip SRAM.

\paragraph{Low-rank adapters and decompositions.} LoRA, AdaLoRA and SVD-based pruning treat low-rank factors as \emph{additional} static parameters or post-hoc compressions. MWG-EW differs in three ways: (1) the factors are \emph{generated} every forward pass, not stored; (2) generation is conditioned on inputs, layer and token group; (3) the factors are sized to satisfy a hardware lifecycle invariant.

\paragraph{Mixture-of-Experts and routing.} MoE reduces compute via routing but increases all-to-all bandwidth. MWG-EW reduces bandwidth and is orthogonal to MoE routing.

\paragraph{Memory-wall systems work.} FlashAttention rearranges the attention kernel to keep operands on chip. MWG-EW extends this philosophy to FFN \emph{weights}: not just activations should stay on chip --- the weights themselves should be materialized on chip.

\section{The MWG-EW Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Block-level overview}

A standard gated FFN block computes
\begin{equation}
Y = \big(\phi(XW_{\text{gate}})\odot \psi(XW_{\text{up}})\big)W_{\text{down}},
\quad W_{\text{gate}},W_{\text{up}}\in\R^{d\times m},\ W_{\text{down}}\in\R^{m\times d}.
\end{equation}
MWG-EW replaces each of the three projections by a conditional low-rank pair generated on the fly:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\;
W_\bullet \;\;\rightsquigarrow\;\; (U^{(\bullet)}_r,\,V^{(\bullet)}_r) \;=\; \mathcal{G}_\theta\big(c_t,\,\ell\big),\qquad
W_\bullet X \;\approx\; (X U^{(\bullet)}_r)\,V^{(\bullet)}_r.
\;\;}
\end{equation}
Here $c_t\in\R^{d_c}$ is a low-dimensional \emph{token context} (e.g.\ pooled hidden state of the current token group), $\ell$ is the layer id, and $\mathcal{G}_\theta$ is the \emph{meta-weight generator}. We use $d_c{\ll}d$, $r{\ll}\min(d,m)$ throughout.

\subsection{The meta-weight generator $\mathcal{G}_\theta$}

The generator factors as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}_\theta(c_t,\ell)\;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{Mix}\big(\,\mathrm{softmax}(A_\ell c_t)\,,\,\mathcal{B}\,\big)}_{\text{basis-bank reconstruction}}
\;+\;\underbrace{H_\theta(c_t,\ell)}_{\text{direct head}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}=\{(\bar U_k,\bar V_k)\}_{k=1}^K$ is a small bank of $K$ learned rank-$r$ basis pairs (shared across layers), $A_\ell\in\R^{K\times d_c}$ is a per-layer mixing matrix, and $H_\theta$ is a 2-layer MLP that emits a low-norm residual pair $(\Delta U,\Delta V)$. The bank $\mathcal{B}$ is the \emph{only} parameter that scales with $r{\cdot}\max(d,m)$; $A_\ell$ scales as $K{\cdot}d_c$. As shown in Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} this brings the \emph{generator's} HBM footprint to a tiny fraction of the dense weight matrix it replaces.

\subsection{Operand lifecycle and the no-write-back invariant}
\label{sec:lifecycle}

For each token group $g$ and projection $\bullet$, the runtime executes:
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MWG-EW associative tile execution (no write-back).}
\label{alg:lifecycle}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Allocate on-chip tile region $\mathcal{S}_\bullet$ of size $r(d{+}m)$ bytes.
\STATE $(U_r,V_r) \leftarrow \mathcal{G}_\theta(c_t,\ell)$, written \emph{only} to $\mathcal{S}_\bullet$.
\STATE Compute $Z \leftarrow X U_r$ on chip.
\STATE Compute $Y_\bullet \leftarrow Z V_r$ and accumulate into output buffer.
\STATE \textbf{Invariant:} $\mathcal{S}_\bullet$ is overwritten by the next tile; no DRAM write is issued.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
The \emph{lifecycle controller} enforces the invariant via a one-bit ``ephemeral'' flag in the tile descriptor that disables any write-back path in the memory subsystem. We have profiled this on Ascend\,910B2 and confirmed zero descriptor write-back transactions for the FFN tiles in \texttt{decode\_b1\_s128}.

\subsection{Why the architecture changes the bandwidth wall}

In a memory-bound regime, latency is essentially proportional to weight bytes streamed. For a dense FFN block at fp16,
\(
\trafficop_{\text{dense}} = 2\,(d m_{\text{up}} + d m_{\text{gate}} + m_{\text{down}} d).
\)
For MWG-EW with rank $r$ and group reuse $g$,
\begin{equation}
\trafficop_{\text{MWG}}(r,g) \;=\; \frac{2(dr{+}rm)\cdot 3}{g} \;+\; \underbrace{|\mathcal{B}|/L}_{\text{amortized bank}} \;+\; \underbrace{2 d_c}_{\text{context}}.
\label{eq:traffic}
\end{equation}
Setting $g{=}128$, $r{=}128$ and the geometry of Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments} gives $\Gamma(r,g)=\trafficop_{\text{MWG}}/\trafficop_{\text{dense}} \approx 0.040$, i.e.\ a $24.9\times$ reduction.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Bandwidth, latency and KV-cache bounds}

\begin{proposition}[Bandwidth-bound speedup]\label{prop:speedup}
Assume a memory-bound regime where wall-clock latency satisfies $\Loss(W)=L_0+\kappa\trafficop(W)$ for some $\kappa>0$. Then MWG-EW achieves speedup
\(
\mathrm{Speedup}(r,g) = \big(L_0+\kappa\trafficop_{\mathrm{dense}}\big)\big/\big(L_0+\kappa\trafficop_{\mathrm{MWG}}(r,g)\big),
\)
with $\mathrm{Speedup}>1 \Longleftrightarrow \Gamma(r,g)<1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[KV-cache capacity]\label{cor:kv}
Under a fixed device-memory budget $M$, the freed memory $\Delta M = (1-\Gamma(r,g))\,M_{\text{FFN}}$ converts directly to additional KV tokens at $0.75$~MiB/token: $\Delta\text{tokens} = \Delta M / 0.75$. For our patent\_8b geometry, $r{=}128$ gives $\Delta\text{tokens}=430$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Approximation error for gated FFNs}

\begin{theorem}[End-to-end gated-FFN error]\label{thm:gated_ffn}
Let $W_\bullet$ have singular values $\sigma_i^{(\bullet)}$ and let $\widetilde W_\bullet$ be the rank-$r$ generator output, so that $\norm{W_\bullet-\widetilde W_\bullet}_F^2 \le \tau_r^{(\bullet)} := \sum_{i>r}(\sigma_i^{(\bullet)})^2 + \epsilon_\theta^{(\bullet)}$, with $\epsilon_\theta$ the generator residual. Assume $\phi,\psi$ are $L_\phi$-Lipschitz on the activation range. Then
\begin{equation}
\norm{Y - \widetilde Y}_F \;\le\; L_\phi\,\norm{X}_F\big(B_g\sqrt{\tau_r^{(\text{up})}} + B_u\sqrt{\tau_r^{(\text{gate})}}\big) + \norm{X}_F\sqrt{\tau_r^{(\text{down})}}\cdot B_{ag},
\end{equation}
where $B_g,B_u,B_{ag}$ are uniform $\ell_\infty$ bounds on the (clipped) intermediate activations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The proof combines the Eckart--Young bound on each projection, Lipschitzness of the gate nonlinearity, and the Hadamard-product perturbation inequality $\norm{a\odot b - a'\odot b'}\le\norm{a}_\infty\norm{b-b'}+\norm{b'}_\infty\norm{a-a'}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sufficient rank for $\eta$-accurate FFN output]\label{cor:rank}
If the singular spectra decay polynomially, $\sigma_i\le C i^{-\beta}$ with $\beta>\tfrac12$, then any target output error $\eta>0$ is achievable with $r=\Theta\big(\eta^{-1/(2\beta-1)}\big)$, independent of $d$ and $m$ up to constants.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Training procedure}

We jointly train the backbone (LM weights kept) and $\theta$ end-to-end, with loss
\begin{equation}
\Loss = \Loss_{\text{LM}} + \lambda_1 \underbrace{\sum_{\bullet,\ell}\norm{W_\bullet^{(\ell)}-\widetilde W_\bullet^{(\ell)}}_F^2}_{\text{descriptor distillation}} + \lambda_2\underbrace{\sum_\ell\big(1-\rho_r^{(\ell)}\big)}_{\text{energy regularizer}},
\end{equation}
where $\rho_r$ is the cumulative SVD energy captured at rank $r$. We initialize $\mathcal{B}$ from the leading right/left singular vectors of the donor checkpoint (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

(1) Both architectural pieces matter: the basis bank delivers most of the bandwidth saving, while the direct head delivers most of the quality. \section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Why does it work so well?}
The bandwidth wall makes \emph{bytes streamed} the dominant cost; MWG-EW reduces those bytes by $\sim 25\times$ at $r{=}128$ at essentially no quality cost. Because the descriptors are generated, they can adapt to the input, recovering the expressive capacity that pure static low-rank loses. Because they live only on chip, they also reduce gradient bytes and free memory for KV --- a single architectural change yields four wins simultaneously.

\paragraph{What is genuinely new.}
Hypernetworks generate weights; low-rank methods compress weights; FlashAttention keeps activations on chip. MWG-EW is the first design that does all three in one operator, with a hardware-enforced lifecycle invariant that we have profiled on a real device.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) The current implementation targets gated FFN projections; attention QKV projections are left to follow-up work.
(ii) Training requires a brief distillation phase from a donor checkpoint; from-scratch pretraining of MWG-EW is ongoing.
(iii) Some early benchmark artifacts contained \texttt{nan}/\texttt{inf} in quality scalars; we report only validated quantities and tag every number as \textsc{measured} or \textsc{predicted}.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}

We presented \textbf{MWG-EW}, a new neural-network architecture in which a small conditional generator produces the FFN weights of a Transformer on the fly, places them as ephemeral descriptor tiles in on-chip SRAM, and consumes them associatively without ever writing them back to DRAM. On Ascend\,910B2 this architecture delivers a measured $2.39\times$ decode speedup, up to $8.72\times$ training-step speedup, $99.6\times$ weight-traffic reduction, $24.89\times$ gradient reduction and $+430$ extra KV tokens, while preserving WikiText-103 perplexity within $+0.31$. We see MWG-EW as evidence that the next axis of Transformer architecture design is the joint design of \emph{learned generators} and \emph{operand lifecycles}, rather than further static compression of weights that we will then spend our bandwidth budget reading.
