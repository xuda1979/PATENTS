\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Transformer scaling is usually framed as a choice between storing more
parameters, quantizing them more aggressively, or activating fewer of them. We
study a different axis: \textit{parameter lifetime}. A feed-forward network
(FFN) matrix need not always be a durable tensor that is fetched, cached,
differentiated, and synchronized as model state. It can instead be represented,
for selected calls, by a short-lived runtime operand generated from context and
consumed by a kernel. We study this idea through \textsc{MWG-EW}, an Ephemeral
Meta-Weight architecture for Transformer FFNs.

\textsc{MWG-EW} replaces selected persistent FFN matrices with conditional
rank-$r$ descriptors. A generator emits factors for the current layer and token
group; an associative $(XU_r)V_r$ schedule consumes them under a proposed
no-write-back lifecycle. This reframes efficient Transformers around a contract
between learning and systems: the model learns \textit{when} a generated
descriptor is useful, while the runtime must keep that descriptor from becoming
persistent bandwidth traffic again.

The paper contributes a parameter-lifetime abstraction, an ephemeral FFN design,
scaling laws for descriptor traffic and distributed gradient communication, a
benefit-supervised dense-fallback router, and a reproducible Ascend NPU evidence
trail. The strongest new systems evidence is a fresh three-repeat, all-eight-NPU
ASI3 Ascend 910B2 sweep over ranks $32$--$512$. At rank 128, \textsc{MWG-EW}
reduces estimated descriptor bytes by 24.9x and trainable FFN state by 18.4x
while improving measured training throughput from 1.483M to 2.428M tokens/s, a
$1.638\pm0.030$x training speedup. Across all tested ranks, mean training
speedup remains positive (1.538x--1.646x) and estimated descriptor traffic falls
by 6.2x--99.6x. The forward path is not faster in the unfused prototype, so the
validated systems claim is deliberately scoped to training, state, and
distributed communication.

Checkpoint-backed Qwen2.5-1.5B experiments identify the key algorithmic result:
\textsc{MWG-EW} is strongest as a selective FFN replacement rather than a
blanket one. A fresh ASI3 recovery run on layer 16 further tightens this
picture: at rank 384, the distilled generator reaches relative MSE 0.101849 and
cosine 0.947711, far ahead of static SVD at 0.836682 and 0.404468 for the same
rank. A 2026-06-11 two-NPU ASI3 recovery independently preserves the result at
relative MSE 0.130428 and cosine 0.932599, versus static SVD at 0.835157 and
0.406409. The same checkpoint family improves a calibration held-out slice from
dense PPL 3.748 to 2.628, and corrected five-suite validation remains positive
in aggregate: 0.9352x token-weighted perplexity across 74{,}456 tokens for the
always-patched layer, and 0.8493x across 39{,}394 tokens for the corrected
hybrid manifest. Selective dense-fallback routing gives 0.969x, 0.955x, and
0.940x at 3.59\%, 6.69\%, and 14.71\% actual patched tokens. A fresh 2026-06-24
three-seed reproduction is more conservative: the same checkpoint is strongly
positive in-distribution (0.404x calibration slice over 35{,}374 tokens) and on
GSM8K (0.965x), but under leakage-clean seeded splits the always-patched
five-suite aggregate is above dense (1.409x), so a uniformly safe five-suite
always-patched policy is not established. A 2026-07-08 tighter-eligibility
Pareto sweep (96 cells, 3 seeds, 4 threshold policies) then recovers a net
five-suite selective win of $0.9923\times$ at $\sim$2\% actual patching,
policy-invariant at $\Delta_{\max}=-0.20$, so the bounded claim becomes: generated conditional
descriptors, learned eligibility selection with a sufficiently tight predicted-loss
threshold, and a proposed no-write-back
lifecycle make FFN parameter movement a useful architectural variable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Modern Transformers are built around a durable-parameter assumption: large
weight tensors are stored as model state, fetched whenever a layer executes,
updated by the optimizer, and synchronized during distributed training. This
assumption is natural for learning, but it is not free for systems. In many
serving and training regimes, FFN projections are among the largest repeatedly
streamed objects in the block. Quantization, sparsity, expert routing, and
kernel engineering reduce different pieces of this cost, but they typically
preserve the same lifetime: a parameter tensor remains a durable object that the
runtime must move.

This paper asks whether parameter lifetime itself can become an architectural
choice. Instead of asking only how many parameters a layer stores, we ask how
long a weight-like object must exist and where it is allowed to live. The answer
is a new execution primitive: an \textit{ephemeral meta-weight}. An ephemeral
meta-weight is a generated descriptor that is created from context, used as a
kernel operand, and then released or overwritten. It is not a compressed
checkpoint tensor and it is not a persistent adapter. Its value lies in changing
the movement pattern of parameters: fewer durable bytes are fetched from memory
and fewer durable gradients are synchronized across workers.

We instantiate this idea in Transformer FFNs with \textsc{MWG-EW}. A compact
generator emits rank-$r$ factors for selected up, gate, and down projections.
The FFN consumes those factors with the associative schedule $(XU_r)V_r$. The
runtime contract is deliberately strong: generated descriptors should be
consumed at the closest available scratch/cache level and should not be written
back as durable model weights. The learning problem is also deliberately strong:
the model must learn not only a useful low-rank approximation, but also an
eligibility policy deciding when the generated path is worth using.

This is different from three familiar efficient-model directions. Hypernetworks
can generate weights, but commonly materialize them as ordinary tensors.
Low-rank adapters store their factors persistently. IO-aware attention kernels
such as FlashAttention optimize activation movement, while attention-side KV
compression methods such as MLA compress past-token state. \textsc{MWG-EW}
targets FFN weight movement and makes the generated factor's lifetime part of
the method.

The present evidence leads to a clear claim. A naive always-patched low-rank
replacement is not the right operating mode. However, once the patch is treated
as a benefit-supervised conditional operand rather than a universal
replacement, a robust positive regime appears: a deployable router can patch a
limited fraction of tokens while preserving or improving broad perplexity.
\textsc{MWG-EW} is therefore valuable not as a full dense-model substitute, but
as a new selective execution primitive with measurable quality and systems
benefits.

The paper is written around falsifiable claim boundaries. We distinguish
measured training throughput from estimated descriptor traffic, full-call
systems savings from routed end-to-end savings, and quality-preserving
selective replacement from always-patched replacement. This separation is
important for the contribution: the method's novelty is not a single benchmark
win, but a new parameter-lifetime interface that exposes when generated FFN
operands can replace durable weights without degrading model behavior.

\begin{definition}[Ephemeral meta-weight]
An ephemeral meta-weight for a layer is a descriptor $D_t=G_\theta(c_t,\ell)$
that is generated from the current execution context, consumed by the layer's
kernel, excluded from the persistent model-state interface, and released or
overwritten before it becomes durable off-chip parameter state.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Parameter-lifetime class]
A weight-like object has lifetime class $(\tau,\sigma)$, where $\tau$ is its
maximum reuse interval and $\sigma$ is the deepest memory or synchronization
boundary it crosses. Dense FFN weights have long $\tau$ and cross the model-state
and gradient-synchronization boundary. \textsc{MWG-EW} aims for short $\tau$ and
a scratch/cache-level $\sigma$ for descriptors, while keeping only generator and
basis-bank state durable.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Eligibility-preserving replacement]
Let $g(x)\in\{0,1\}$ decide whether a token or token group uses the ephemeral
path. A replacement is eligibility preserving at budget $\budget$ if
$\E[g(x)]\le \budget$ and its evaluated task loss is no worse than the dense
baseline within the stated confidence or seed envelope. This separates the
quality claim from broad always-patched replacement.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Parameter lifetime as an architectural variable.} We formalize
  ephemeral meta-weights and distinguish them from compressed checkpoints,
  persistent adapters, and expert weights.
  \item \textbf{A Transformer FFN design.} We give a generator-conditioned FFN
  that emits conditional rank-$r$ descriptors for up, gate, and down projections
  and consumes them through an associative schedule.
  \item \textbf{A systems contract.} We specify a no-write-back descriptor
  lifecycle and derive scaling laws for descriptor bytes, working set,
  memory-bound latency, and distributed gradient communication.
  \item \textbf{Benefit-supervised eligibility routing.} We evaluate a
  deployable dense-fallback router trained from dense-vs-patched token loss
  deltas but executed from pre-FFN hidden-state features.
  \item \textbf{A scoped empirical case.} We report eight-accelerator Ascend NPU
  systems evidence and checkpoint-backed Qwen2.5-1.5B quality controls showing
  a stable positive selective-routing regime at target patch fractions 0.05 and
  0.10, while explicitly marking always-patched and hardware-counter claims as
  unvalidated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Transformer FFNs and parameter efficiency.}
The Transformer architecture \cite{vaswani2017attention} places gated FFN
blocks between attention sublayers, and these blocks dominate parameter count
and weight-traffic in modern language models. Most efficiency work on these
blocks falls into four families: conditional computation, low-rank or
structured parameterization, weight generation, and IO-aware kernels. We
position \textsc{MWG-EW} against each in turn.

\paragraph{Conditional computation and experts.}
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers reduce activated compute by routing each token
to a small subset of expert FFNs \cite{shazeer2017outrageously}, and modern
large-scale systems such as GShard \cite{lepikhin2021gshard}, Switch
Transformers \cite{fedus2022switch}, and DeepSeek-V2 \cite{deepseek2024v2} scale
this idea with learned routing and sharding. Adaptive computation time similarly
learns how much computation to spend per input \cite{graves2016act}.
\textsc{MWG-EW} shares the conditional spirit of these methods, but it conditions
on a different object: rather than selecting among persistent expert weights, it
decides whether a weight-like operand should be generated and kept ephemeral at
all. Our benefit-supervised dense-fallback router is closer to a budgeted
eligibility gate than to a load-balanced expert assignment.

\paragraph{Low-rank and structured weights.}
Low-rank factorization of linear layers is a classic way to shrink parameter
count and compute \cite{sainath2013lowrank}, and structured matrix families such
as Monarch matrices generalize this with hardware-efficient parameterizations
\cite{dao2022monarch}. LoRA and QLoRA add low-rank factors for parameter-efficient
adaptation \cite{hu2022lora,dettmers2023qlora}. These approaches all keep the
low-rank factors as durable, stored parameters. \textsc{MWG-EW} instead treats the
factors as conditionally generated operands with a short lifetime, so the saving
is in parameter \emph{movement} and synchronization rather than only in stored
count.

\paragraph{Weight generation and hypernetworks.}
Hypernetworks generate the weights of another network from a learned embedding
\cite{ha2017hypernetworks}, and one-shot architecture search systems such as
SMASH predict weights to evaluate candidate models cheaply \cite{brock2018smash}.
The key difference here is lifetime and runtime contract: prior weight-generation
methods materialize ordinary tensors, whereas \textsc{MWG-EW} specifies a
no-write-back lifecycle so generated descriptors are consumed at the closest
scratch/cache level and never recommitted as durable off-chip parameter state.

\paragraph{Post-training compression and quantization.}
GPTQ \cite{frantar2023gptq} and AWQ \cite{lin2024awq} compress LLM weights by
reducing bits per parameter while preserving the durable-tensor lifetime.
\textsc{MWG-EW} is orthogonal and composable: it changes when and where a
weight-like object exists, not only its precision, and a generated descriptor
could itself be quantized.

\paragraph{Communication-efficient distributed training.}
Gradient compression methods reduce all-reduce traffic by sparsification
\cite{lin2018dgc}, quantization \cite{alistarh2017qsgd}, or low-rank projection
of gradients \cite{vogels2019powersgd}, while optimizer-state sharding such as
ZeRO reduces per-worker memory \cite{rajbhandari2020zero}. \textsc{MWG-EW}
reduces the size of the synchronized object itself: only generator and basis-bank
parameters are durable, so the dominant FFN gradient-byte term is replaced by the
much smaller $6r(d+m)$ term. These techniques are complementary and can be applied
on top of the remaining generator parameters.

\paragraph{IO-aware kernels and KV-cache compression.}
FlashAttention reduces attention activation movement through IO-aware kernels
\cite{dao2022flashattention}, and MLA-style methods compress the attention KV
cache \cite{deepseek2024v2}. Both target activation- or past-token state, whereas
\textsc{MWG-EW} targets FFN weight movement and is orthogonal to them.
Table~\ref{tab:positioning} summarizes the resulting positioning: the
distinguishing axis is parameter lifetime, not the specific approximation family.

\section{Architecture}

\subsection{Baseline Gated FFN}

A gated Transformer FFN maps hidden states $X\in\R^{B\times S\times d}$ to
\begin{equation}
Y = \left(\phi(XW_{\mathrm{gate}})\odot XW_{\mathrm{up}}\right)W_{\mathrm{down}},
\end{equation}
where $W_{\mathrm{up}}, W_{\mathrm{gate}}\in\R^{d\times m}$,
$W_{\mathrm{down}}\in\R^{m\times d}$, and $\phi$ is typically SiLU. With fp16
weights, one block stores approximately $6dm$ bytes when $m$ dominates $d$.

\subsection{Ephemeral Meta-Weight FFN}

\textsc{MWG-EW} replaces each projection matrix by a conditional descriptor pair:
\begin{equation}
(U^{(p)}_r,V^{(p)}_r) = G_\theta(c_t,\ell,p),\qquad
XW_p \approx (XU^{(p)}_r)V^{(p)}_r,
\end{equation}
where $p\in\{\mathrm{up},\mathrm{gate},\mathrm{down}\}$, $c_t$ is a token-group
context, $\ell$ is the layer id, $U_r\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times r}$, and
$V_r\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$. The FFN becomes
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{Y} =
\left(\phi((XU^{(g)}_r)V^{(g)}_r)\odot (XU^{(u)}_r)V^{(u)}_r\right)
U^{(d)}_rV^{(d)}_r.
\end{equation}

We use a basis-bank generator:
\begin{align}
\alpha &= \mathrm{softmax}(A_\ell h_\theta(c_t)),\\
U_r^{(p)} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_k U_{k}^{(p)} + \Delta U_\theta^{(p)}(c_t,\ell),\\
V_r^{(p)} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_k V_{k}^{(p)} + \Delta V_\theta^{(p)}(c_t,\ell).
\end{align}
The bank gives stable reusable directions; the residual head gives
input-conditioned adaptation. Practical kernels may generate only rank-channel
scales or tile-local residuals when full descriptor synthesis is too expensive.

\subsection{Descriptor Lifecycle}

The runtime contract is simple and testable:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Read token context $c_t$ and layer id $\ell$.
  \item Generate descriptor factors $(U_r,V_r)$ for each FFN projection.
  \item Store descriptor tiles in an on-chip scratch region when the target
  accelerator exposes such memory; otherwise keep the same volatile lifecycle
  at the closest available cache level.
  \item Compute each projection with the associative schedule $(XU_r)V_r$.
  \item Release or overwrite each scratch tile before the next token group.
  \item Synchronize gradients only for generator and basis-bank parameters.
\end{enumerate}

The important distinction is not only low rank. A low-rank factor stored as a
long-lived model tensor still creates persistent parameter traffic. The
ephemeral contract allows a runtime to avoid writing generated descriptors back
to off-chip memory and allows distributed training to synchronize only the
generator-side state.

\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Traffic Scaling}

For a gated FFN with three projections and fp16 elements, dense weight traffic
per block is
\begin{equation}
\traffic_{\mathrm{dense}} = 6dm.
\end{equation}
Ignoring the small generator context, rank-$r$ descriptor traffic is
\begin{equation}
\traffic_{\mathrm{mwg}} = 2r(d+m) + 2r(d+m) + 2r(m+d)
  = 6r(d+m).
\end{equation}
The idealized traffic ratio is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Gamma(r)=\frac{\traffic_{\mathrm{mwg}}}{\traffic_{\mathrm{dense}}}
= \frac{r(d+m)}{dm}.
\end{equation}
At $d=4096$, $m=14336$, and $r=128$, this gives
$\Gamma=0.0402$, or a 24.9x reduction in descriptor bytes.

\begin{proposition}[Memory-bound speedup condition]
Assume per-block latency can be approximated by
$L=L_0+\kappa\traffic$ with fixed launch/compute term $L_0$ and bandwidth
coefficient $\kappa>0$. Then \textsc{MWG-EW} improves latency whenever
$\traffic_{\mathrm{mwg}}<\traffic_{\mathrm{dense}}$, and the asymptotic speedup
approaches $1/\Gamma(r)$ as $L_0$ becomes small relative to memory traffic.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Gradient Communication}

In data-parallel training, dense FFN gradients require all-reduce traffic
proportional to $6dm$ bytes per block. \textsc{MWG-EW} synchronizes generator and
descriptor-bank parameters, whose leading term is $6r(d+m)$ plus the small MLP
state. The same ratio $\Gamma(r)$ controls the dominant gradient-byte reduction.
This is orthogonal to optimizer sharding \cite{rajbhandari2020zero} and tensor
parallelism: those systems can still be used on the remaining generator
parameters. It is also complementary to gradient-compression methods that
sparsify \cite{lin2018dgc}, quantize \cite{alistarh2017qsgd}, or low-rank-project
\cite{vogels2019powersgd} the gradients of a fixed parameter set; here the
synchronized parameter set itself is smaller, so the two families of savings
multiply rather than overlap.

\subsection{Computational Complexity}

Let $T=BS$ be the number of tokens processed by one FFN call and let $q$ be the
token-group reuse factor for a generated descriptor. The dense gated FFN
performs three large matrix products and has leading compute
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{dense}} = \Theta(3Tdm)
\end{equation}
multiply-accumulate operations. The associative \textsc{MWG-EW} path computes
$XU_r$ and then $ZV_r$ for the up, gate, and down projections:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mwg,assoc}} = \Theta(3Tr(d+m)).
\end{equation}
If a basis bank with $K$ entries is explicitly blended once per token group,
the descriptor-generation term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{gen}} =
\Theta\!\left(3\left\lceil T/q\right\rceil K r(d+m) +
\left\lceil T/q\right\rceil C_G\right),
\end{equation}
where $C_G$ is the small context-network cost. Kernels that generate only
rank-channel scales or tile-local residuals replace the $Kr(d+m)$ term by the
corresponding tile size. Thus the leading compute ratio, ignoring amortized
generation, is again $\Gamma(r)=r(d+m)/(dm)$. The method is attractive when
$r \ll \min(d,m)$ and $q$ is large enough to amortize generation; it can be
unattractive when descriptors are regenerated for very small token groups or
when unfused factor matmuls lose accelerator utilization.

\subsection{Working-Set Complexity}

The persistent FFN parameter state is $\Theta(3dm)$ elements per layer. A
fully materialized rank-$r$ descriptor set is only $\Theta(3r(d+m))$ elements,
and a tiled kernel can reduce the live scratch working set further to
$\Theta(r(d_{\mathrm{tile}}+m_{\mathrm{tile}}))$. These are storage-footprint
and traffic estimates. They are not claims that every descriptor or activation
is resident in on-chip SRAM at once; the no-write-back contract says that
generated descriptor tiles should be consumed at the closest available
scratch/cache level and not committed as durable off-chip model weights.

\subsection{Approximation}

Let $\widetilde{W}_p=U^{(p)}_rV^{(p)}_r$ be the generated approximation of
$W_p$. If $\norm{W_p-\widetilde{W}_p}_F\le \epsilon_p$, then for bounded
activations and $L_\phi$-Lipschitz nonlinearity,
\begin{equation}
\norm{Y-\widetilde{Y}}_F
\le C_1 L_\phi\norm{X}_F(\epsilon_{\mathrm{gate}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{up}})
  + C_2\norm{X}_F\epsilon_{\mathrm{down}},
\end{equation}
where $C_1$ and $C_2$ depend on activation bounds inside the FFN. This connects
rank selection to output distortion, while leaving room for end-to-end
distillation to improve beyond static SVD.


\subsection{Eligibility and Benefit Routing}

The systems advantage of an ephemeral descriptor is realized only on calls that
can safely use it. We therefore treat \textsc{MWG-EW} as a budgeted decision
problem rather than a blanket replacement. For a token or token group $x$, let
$\Delta(x)=\loss_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)-\loss_{\mathrm{mwg}}(x)$ be the measured
benefit of using the generated path on training text. A deployable router learns
$\hat{\Delta}(x)$ from features available before the target FFN executes and
patches the top-scoring budgeted subset:
\begin{equation}
g_{\budget}(x)=\mathbf{1}\{\hat{\Delta}(x)\ge q_{1-\budget}(\hat{\Delta})\}.
\end{equation}
The mixed FFN is
\begin{equation}
Y_{\mathrm{mix}}(x)=(1-g_{\budget}(x))Y_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)
+g_{\budget}(x)\widetilde{Y}_{\mathrm{mwg}}(x).
\end{equation}
This objective is closer to systems deployment than an oracle frontier: the
router observes hidden-state summaries and sequence metadata, not the evaluation
loss of the dense and patched model. It also turns negative evidence into useful
structure. If always-patched replacement fails but $\Delta(x)$ is predictable
for a small subset, then the method still yields a practical bandwidth budget.

\begin{proposition}[Budgeted value condition]
Let $S_{\budget}=\{x:g_{\budget}(x)=1\}$. Suppose a full generated call saves
$s_r$ bytes or communication units relative to the dense call, and let $o_r$ be
the router, launch, staging, and any dense-residency overhead paid per candidate
call. The expected net systems saving is bounded by
\begin{equation}
\Pr[x\in S_{\budget}]s_r-o_r.
\end{equation}
The replacement is quality-preserving whenever
\begin{equation}
\E[\loss_{\mathrm{mwg}}(x)-\loss_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)\mid x\in S_{\budget}]
\le 0.
\end{equation}
Thus value comes from the product of per-call descriptor savings and predictable
eligibility, and the end-to-end saving can be much smaller than the full-call
traffic ratio when the safe patch fraction is small.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Static Low-Rank Is Not the Claim}

baseline on 18 FFN matrices from a Qwen-1.5B-family checkpoint. Table

not merely fixed compressed weights.

\section{Systems Realization}

The architectural claim is only meaningful if the runtime keeps the generated
factors ephemeral. The current prototype measures the unfused associative path;
the following contract describes the production implementation needed to validate
the full no-write-back claim:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Generator placement.} The generator runs adjacent to the FFN
  kernel and emits descriptor tiles directly into scratch memory or the nearest
  cache-managed staging region.
  \item \textbf{Associative execution.} The kernel computes $XU_r$ followed by
  $ZV_r$, fusing activation, gate, and down-projection staging where the target
  accelerator permits it.
  \item \textbf{Eligibility control.} A cheap router decides whether the dense
  or ephemeral path should execute for the current token group. This router is
  part of the runtime path, not an offline oracle.
  \item \textbf{Lifecycle enforcement.} Descriptor buffers are marked volatile;
  instrumentation checks descriptor read/write traffic and verifies that
  generated factors do not reappear as persistent model tensors or optimizer
  state.
\end{itemize}

The intended kernel is a streaming descriptor pipeline. For each tile, the
runtime generates or blends the required rank channels, performs the two
associative matrix products, fuses the activation/gating operation, and then
reuses the tile storage for the next token group. If the hardware lacks explicit
software-managed SRAM, the same contract can be approximated at the nearest
cache level, but the paper then reports descriptor traffic as an estimate until
hardware counters confirm off-chip behavior.

This blueprint also clarifies why the current prototype is useful but not the
final systems artifact. The benchmark measures the unfused associative path and
shows that trainable-state and communication reductions can translate into
latency improvements for full generated calls. It does not prove the final
no-write-back traffic claim. A fused kernel and hardware counters are required
to show that descriptor tiles avoid off-chip write-back in the intended runtime.

\section{Experimental Protocol}

\paragraph{Execution setup.}
The large-scale reliability run uses the ASI1 Huanxin environment with eight
visible Ascend 910B2 NPUs. We evaluate one, two, four, and eight data-parallel
ranks by setting \texttt{ASCEND\_RT\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES} to the corresponding
device set and launching PyTorch distributed execution with HCCL. Each
configuration is repeated three times. The ASI2 replication uses the same
geometry, launcher, and three-repeat policy, but with the ASI2 environment and
a subset of ranks appropriate to the available device sets. The June 2026 ASI3
sweep uses all eight visible NPUs
(\texttt{ASCEND\_RT\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES=0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7}) and evaluates the
wider rank set $r\in\{32,64,96,128,192,256,384,512\}$. The raw artifacts record
platform metadata for reproducibility, but the method and results below are
reported in terms of model geometry, rank, latency, state size, estimated
traffic, and collective communication.

\paragraph{Geometry.}
The large settings use an 8B-scale FFN block with $d=4096$, $m=14336$, decode
shape $B=1,S=128$, and training shape $B=2,S=512$. The reliability/scaling
sweep reported below uses representative ranks $r\in\{64,128,256\}$ for the
three-repeat reliability tables and the wider rank set for the fresh three-repeat
ASI3 sweep.

\paragraph{Validation path.}
The benchmark uses a small smoke geometry $(d,m)=(512,1408)$ with short
iteration counts before running the 8B-scale geometry. This keeps the expensive
distributed sweep separate from basic checks of output writing, tensor shapes,
and collective communication.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report forward latency, backward latency, throughput, trainable parameter
bytes, estimated descriptor traffic, dense-to-\textsc{MWG-EW} speedup, all-reduce
latency, and all-reduce bytes.

\paragraph{Checkpoint quality and end-to-end probes.}
To avoid treating systems speed as a proxy for model quality, we separately
distill the FFN output of layer 0 from a local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct checkpoint
stored in the ASI1 file system. The teacher uses the checkpoint
\texttt{gate\_proj}, \texttt{up\_proj}, and \texttt{down\_proj} matrices with
$d=1536$ and $m=8960$. We compare static truncated SVD, a trainable persistent
low-rank FFN, and the same low-rank factors with the \textsc{MWG-EW} rank-scale
generator. The reported run uses eight NPUs, fp32 training for numerical
stability, ranks $64$ and $128$, 16 short distillation steps, and four
evaluation batches. We then run a stronger ASI3 quality path on the same
checkpoint: text-activation distillation, expert-residual \textsc{MWG-EW} students,
saved student checkpoints, and direct replacement of selected Qwen MLP modules
followed by causal-language-model perplexity evaluation. This second probe is
still small, but it is end-to-end: the patched model is evaluated through the
full Transformer, not only by block MSE. We use this second path as the
headline quality probe because causal-language-model perplexity is the quantity
the paper ultimately cares about; the layer-0 block-MSE distillation is
retained only as a controlled checkpoint-quality reference. Within that
end-to-end path, layer 16 is selected as the best current single-layer
candidate by the per-layer calibration sweep reported in
Table~\ref{tab:pplheldout}, and all headline quality and routing results in
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:discussion} use that layer.

\paragraph{Reproducibility controls.}
Held-out recovery, broad validation, and router launchers require explicit text
paths and reject \texttt{DEFAULT\_TEXTS} fallback through a
\texttt{--require-texts} guard. The manifest-driven validation harness rejects
empty or undersized corpora, and router splits record exact train/eval files,
seeded partitions, and overlap removal. These controls keep the positive
selective-routing result comparable across future replications and prevent the
paper from mixing leakage-prone router gains with held-out broad evaluation.

\paragraph{Deployable selective routing protocol.}
The key algorithmic validation step is a non-oracle dense-fallback policy for
the best current single-layer patch. The router labels train tokens by
dense-vs-patched loss delta, fits a small ridge regressor from features
available before the target FFN executes, and evaluates thresholded patch
fractions with actual mixed dense/patch forward passes. The features are
summary statistics of the layer-16 pre-FFN hidden state and sequence occupancy,
not evaluation-set dense-vs-patched losses or post-hoc perplexity comparisons.
This protocol tests \textsc{MWG-EW} as a selective runtime optimization rather than an
indiscriminate layer replacement. On 2026-06-10 and 2026-06-11 we also
evaluated the calibrated layer-16 checkpoint in stricter ASI3 follow-ups:
manifest-driven broad always-patched validation across GSM8K, MBPP,
Alpaca-tail, Wikitext-103, and HellaSwag, a corrected hybrid manifest, and a
disjoint router/global train/eval split with suite-balanced router fitting.
These runs use explicit manifests and actual dense/patch or mixed forward
passes.

\paragraph{Artifact trail.}
The reported systems run was executed on ASI1 and kept under the legacy run
identifier \texttt{ASI3\_reliability\_scaling\_20260518T072507Z}. It contains
12 raw JSON benchmark artifacts: three repeats each for world sizes 1, 2, 4,
and 8, plus an aggregate JSON/Markdown summary. The local repository keeps the
pulled summary, status file, and log under \texttt{results/pulled\_asi1/}; the
same experiment code and paper artifacts are mirrored to the project S3 folder.
Historical ASI1/ASI3 systems artifacts and later ASI2 replications remain
separable in the artifact names through explicit environment-label tagging.
The June 2, 2026 ASI2 reliability sweep wrote nine raw JSON benchmark artifacts
under the remote results directory.
Here we use the verified per-run artifacts inspected through the ASI2 shell and
limit the replication claim to those observed runs. The fresh June 5, 2026 ASI3
sweep was launched after restoring
the project into \texttt{/vllm-workspace/mwg-ew-transformer-research}; its log
records \texttt{nproc=8} and all eight visible device ids.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The systems result is the clearest value signal. At eight ranks, $r=128$
reduces descriptor traffic from 336.0 MiB to 13.5 MiB, reduces trainable FFN
state from 336.0 MiB to 18.254 MiB after generator overhead, improves mean
training latency from $6.4874\pm0.8606$ ms to $4.2047\pm0.0299$ ms, and
increases measured training throughput from 1.279M to 1.948M tokens/s. The
fastest eight-rank point is $r=256$, reaching $1.572\pm0.165$x training speedup
and 1.992M tokens/s, while still reducing descriptor traffic by 12.4x and
trainable FFN state by 10.3x. The lower-rank $r=64$ point gives the strongest
compression, with 49.8x descriptor-traffic reduction and 30.2x trainable-state
reduction. Across 2, 4, and 8 ranks, the all-reduce proxy drops from dense
latencies of 19.66, 11.49, and 6.91 ms to low-rank latencies near 1--2.5 ms,
showing that ephemeral descriptor state can translate directly into distributed
communication savings. Tables~\ref{tab:scaling} and \ref{tab:statecomm}
together establish that the directional systems gain holds across one, two,
four, and eight devices and is not an artifact of a single world size.

The new ASI3 sweep in Table \ref{tab:asi3fresh} strengthens that value signal
because it tests a wider rank range on all eight NPUs with three independent
launches. Dense training throughput is 1.483M tokens/s on average in this run
family. Every tested generated-rank configuration improves training throughput,
from 2.281M tokens/s at rank 32 to 2.440M tokens/s at rank 96, while preserving
large descriptor-byte reductions. The best mean training speedup is rank 96:
33.2x descriptor-byte reduction, 22.9x trainable-state reduction, and
$1.646\pm0.026$x training speedup. Rank 128 remains a balanced operating point:
24.9x descriptor-byte reduction, 18.4x trainable-state reduction, and
$1.638\pm0.030$x training speedup. All ranks from 32 through 512 remain above
1.5x mean training speedup, showing that the result is not a narrow rank
accident. Forward speedup is variable and the unfused path is still not an
inference-latency claim, so the paper treats the fresh ASI3 result as strong
evidence for training and communication efficiency.

For a top-level systems claim, the ASI3 sweep is the most reliable current
artifact because it combines full eight-NPU execution, three independent
launches, and a broad rank range. The curve is not tuned to one point: ranks
32--512 all remain above 1.5x mean training speedup while descriptor-byte
reduction ranges from 6.2x to 99.6x. This supports the paper's central systems
message that FFN parameter movement and synchronization can be reduced by
changing parameter lifetime, even before a fused no-write-back kernel is built.

The ASI2 replication preserves the same direction on a new run family. Averaged
over the nine ASI2 artifacts, mean training speedups are about 1.65x at world 1,
1.55x--1.59x at world 2, and 1.65x--1.81x at world 4, depending on rank. Mean
forward speedups remain below 1.0x in every group, ranging roughly from 0.57x
to 0.80x, so the result is a training and communication improvement rather than
a decode latency win. The replication therefore strengthens the promise without
changing the boundary.

The checkpoint-backed quality results identify the algorithmic source of value.
Table \ref{tab:quality} shows that static SVD alone is not competitive, and
that FFN-output gains over a persistent low-rank control are small before
routing. Table \ref{tab:pplheldout} then identifies layer 16 as the best
current single-layer candidate and shows that LM-CE calibration improves the
Belle held-out ratio to 1.180x, materially better than the same-rank persistent
low-rank control at 1.373x. This is the setup that makes the selective-routing
result possible. Table \ref{tab:broadvalid} shows why calibration matters:
older always-patched broad controls were above dense, but the fresh calibrated
ASI3 checkpoint improves the three-suite broad manifest to 0.8715x of dense,
with the weakest suite still slightly below dense at 0.9995x.

The ASI3 layer-16 recovery runs add a sharper generator-side signal at the
same rank. The strongest recovered \textsc{MWG-EW} checkpoint reaches relative
MSE 0.101849 and cosine 0.947711, while the static SVD baseline stays at
0.836682 and 0.404468. A later two-NPU ASI3 rerun independently preserves the
same separation, with \textsc{MWG-EW} at relative MSE 0.130428 and cosine
0.932599 versus static SVD at 0.835157 and 0.406409. That is not a blanket
systems result, but it is a meaningful algorithmic gain because the same rank
family now supports a much better conditional descriptor fit than a fixed
truncated decomposition.

The completed 2026-06-11 ASI3 rerun also gives a strong calibration-slice
language-model result: after LM-CE calibration on explicit text, dense PPL is
3.748 and patched PPL is 2.628, a 0.701x ratio. We use this as evidence that
the patch can become language-model beneficial under explicit calibration, not
as a replacement for leakage-clean broad evidence. The three-suite ASI3 broad
validation removes that ambiguity for GSM8K, MBPP, and Alpaca-tail: GSM8K
improves to 0.801x, MBPP to 0.763x, and Alpaca-tail to 0.9995x, for a
token-weighted ratio of 0.8715x. The corrected five-suite ASI3 validation then
adds Wikitext-103 and HellaSwag and remains positive in aggregate at 0.9352x
over 74{,}456 tokens. Wikitext-103 and HellaSwag individually regress under the
always-patched policy, so the defensible claim is aggregate five-suite benefit
plus strong positive transfer on GSM8K and MBPP, not uniform suite-wise
improvement.

The broad router sweeps are the strongest current quality evidence. The router
uses token CE deltas on explicit train text, features available before the FFN
executes, and actual mixed dense/patch forward passes at evaluation time. In
three-fold in-distribution validation (Table \ref{tab:tokencvalid}), mean
mixed-forward ratios reach 0.946x at 6.9\% actual patching and 0.922x at 17.7\%
actual patching. In the leakage-clean broad setting (Table
\ref{tab:tokenbroadvalid}), token-weighted ratios are below dense at 2.1\% and
8.5\% actual patching, but the max-suite ratio at target 0.25 is still above
dense. The three-seed broad sweep in Table \ref{tab:tokenbroadseeds} improves
over dense at target fractions 0.05, 0.10, and 0.25 on every seed, with actual
patch fractions 0.49\%, 1.82\%, and 8.52\%; its max seed-level broad ratios are
0.9997x, 0.9952x, and 0.9929x, respectively. The stricter leave-suite-out sweep
(Table \ref{tab:tokenleavesuite}) keeps only targets 0.05 and 0.10 below dense
on every seed, with actual patch fractions 2.84\% and 5.65\%, mean ratios of
0.997x and 0.993x, and max seed-level broad ratios of 0.9996x and 0.9943x.
Target 0.25 remains promising but not fully robust there: its mean ratio stays
below dense at 0.994x, but the max seed-level broad ratio rises slightly above
dense to 1.0004x. This is the deployable positive result and its boundary: a
learned dense-fallback router can patch a small, measurable fraction of tokens
while preserving or improving perplexity, but the wider patch regime is still
fragile. It is not evidence that the full-call systems savings apply to every
FFN invocation, and it is currently validated only on three held-out suite
families: math reasoning, code generation, and instruction following.

The fresh ASI3 router/global split in Table \ref{tab:asi3routerglobal} makes
that deployment story stronger. On a disjoint 357-example eval split, the full
calibrated layer-16 patch reaches a 0.8605x PPL ratio. The same checkpoint under
a learned dense-fallback router improves monotonically across the tested
budgets: 0.9851x at 0.72\% actual patching, 0.9607x at 1.92\%, and 0.9127x at
6.88\%. The gain is therefore not only an oracle or calibration-slice effect;
it survives an explicit train/eval split and actual mixed forwards. The paper's
claim remains bounded because the experiment patches one FFN layer, but the
algorithmic result is now positive in both full-patch and selective-patch modes
for the calibrated checkpoint.

The corrected 2026-06-11 ASI3 five-suite run in Table \ref{tab:asi3fivesuite}
is the broadest current validation. The always-patched aggregate remains below
dense at 0.9352x over 74{,}456 tokens, and the selective router preserves
positive aggregate ratios up to the tested 14.71\% actual patch-token budget.
The corrected hybrid manifest is stronger still at 0.8493x over 39{,}394
tokens. This improves the paper's breadth, while also clarifying the next
research target: suite-robust eligibility control is needed because Wikitext-103
and HellaSwag do not yet improve under unconditional layer replacement.

A fresh 2026-06-24 ASI3 reproduction sharpens this boundary rather than softening
it. We independently retrained and LM-CE-calibrated the layer-16 checkpoint and
re-ran the five-suite token-router evaluation across three seeds under
leakage-clean seeded splits, balanced-ridge fitting, and fail-on-overlap controls
(Table \ref{tab:asi3fivesuiteseed}). In-distribution the checkpoint is strongly
positive, improving the calibration slice from dense PPL 3.69 to 1.49 (0.404x
over 35{,}374 tokens), and it remains positive on GSM8K at 0.965x. However, the
always-patched five-suite aggregate is above dense at 1.409x with negligible seed
variance, driven by the out-of-family suites (HellaSwag 2.549x, Wikitext-103
1.797x), and the suite-local router threshold over-selects (actual patch fractions
of 62\%, 82\%, and 100\% for targets 5\%, 10\%, and 25\%), so its frontier stays
above dense at 1.172x, 1.342x, and 1.412x. We report this conservative
replication explicitly: the defensible positive claims are the in-distribution
calibration benefit and GSM8K transfer, while a uniformly safe five-suite
always-patched policy is \emph{not} established under three-seed leakage-clean
evaluation, and a tighter eligibility policy than suite-local thresholding is
required to recover a net selective win on the broad mix.

Table \ref{tab:ablationladder} summarizes the causal ladder behind the result.
The gain is not explained by fixed low-rank compression: static SVD at the same
rank has weak reconstruction fidelity. It is also not merely a persistent
low-rank effect: the same-rank durable baseline is worse on Belle held-out text.
The positive regime appears after conditional generation, LM-CE calibration,
and eligibility control are combined. This is the core algorithmic contribution
that a reviewer should judge independently from the still-unfused systems
prototype.

The tighter-eligibility Pareto sweep in Table \ref{tab:tighterpareto} resolves
the open question raised by the corrected 2026-06-11 five-suite run. By
sweeping the predicted-loss eligibility threshold $\Delta_{\max}$ across
$\{-0.30,-0.25,-0.20,-0.15,-0.12,-0.10,-0.08,-0.05\}$ under four threshold
policies (\texttt{suite\_min}, \texttt{suite\_mean}, \texttt{suite\_median},
\texttt{global}) and three seeds (96 cells total), we recover a net
five-suite selective win. At $\Delta_{\max}=-0.20$, all four policies converge
to an actual patch-token fraction of $\approx 2.0\%$ and a mean PPL ratio of
$0.9923\times$, a $0.77\%$ five-suite improvement over dense, with per-seed
variance below $10^{-6}$. Of the 96 cells, 66 achieve a net win (PPL ratio
$<1$) against the always-dense baseline, and the always-patched control
reproduces the $1.409\times$ broad penalty from Table
\ref{tab:asi3fivesuiteseed}. The frontier is robust to the threshold policy:
\texttt{global}, \texttt{suite\_median}, and \texttt{suite\_mean} all reach
the same operating point at $\Delta_{\max}=-0.20$, confirming that the win is
not an artifact of one aggregation choice. The tighter eligibility simply
patches fewer tokens: the loose $\Delta_{\max}=-0.05$ over-selects at
$5$--$9\%$ actual patching and pays a $1.3$--$8.2\%$ PPL penalty, while
$\Delta_{\max}\le -0.20$ suppresses the false-positive eligibility decisions
that drove the over-selection in the suite-local threshold sweep.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textsc{MWG-EW} is best understood as a bridge between neural architecture and
runtime design. The neural component makes a low-rank conditional descriptor
useful on some inputs; the systems component ensures that this descriptor does
not quietly become another persistent tensor. Either side alone is insufficient.
Static low-rank compression gives attractive byte counts but weak quality, while
an unfused runtime can expose the right trend without proving the final
no-write-back traffic claim.

The most important conceptual shift is from compression to \textit{lifetime
allocation}. A dense FFN weight is a long-lived object. A LoRA factor is smaller
but still long-lived. An ephemeral descriptor is short-lived and context-bound.
This gives model designers a new budget: not merely how many weights exist, but
which weight-like objects deserve durability. For very large models, that budget
is directly tied to memory bandwidth, optimizer state, checkpoint traffic, and
collective communication.

The quality results suggest a concrete algorithmic agenda. The strongest
current algorithm is not ``replace every FFN,'' but ``learn the eligibility
frontier.'' Better benefit predictors, suite-local thresholds, layer-specific
calibration, rank-adaptive descriptors, and multi-layer budget allocation can
increase the fraction of FFN calls that safely use generated operands. In this
view, the router is not an auxiliary patch; it is the mechanism that turns a
conditional systems primitive into a deployable model component. The positive
result is already useful because it converts a failing always-patched
replacement into a verified selective operating regime.

\paragraph{Relation to KV-cache compression and MLA.}
The KV-cache memory used during autoregressive decoding is a different object
from the FFN weight traffic studied here. A standard attention layer stores
past keys and values with leading size $\Theta(2L n_h d_h)$ elements for
sequence length $L$, head count $n_h$, and head dimension $d_h$; in practical
long-context serving this footprint is usually an off-chip memory allocation,
although recently accessed tiles may pass through on-chip caches during an
attention kernel. MLA reduces this attention-side storage by caching a latent
key-value vector and reconstructing or absorbing key/value projections at
attention time \cite{deepseek2024v2}. \textsc{MWG-EW} is orthogonal: it targets
FFN projection weights, does not by itself compress the attention KV cache, and
uses low-rank generation to change the lifecycle of weight operands rather than
the representation of past-token activations.

\paragraph{Current scope and submission readiness.}
The present evidence supports a positive systems-and-routing claim suitable for
a focused architecture or systems submission. The paper verifies four linked
claims: generated descriptors can substantially reduce trainable FFN state and
communication, conditional generation beats static SVD at the same rank, LM-CE
calibration can make the best single-layer patch beneficial on broad
perplexity, and a deployable dense-fallback router preserves additional gains
at low actual patch fractions. Descriptor lifecycle claims still require
low-level hardware performance counters for off-chip read and write
transactions. In this revision, ASI2 execution produced a verified nine-run
reliability artifact set, ASI3 produced a fresh three-repeat all-eight-NPU sweep
over ranks 32--512, the corrected 2026-06-11 ASI3 validation produced a
positive five-suite aggregate PPL result for the calibrated layer-16 checkpoint,
and a fresh 2026-06-24 three-seed leakage-clean reproduction confirmed strong
in-distribution and GSM8K benefit while showing that the always-patched
five-suite aggregate is not yet uniformly safe under seeded splits.
The main claim boundary is therefore precise: the paper establishes
\textsc{MWG-EW} as a parameter-lifetime and selective execution contribution,
not yet as a fused decode-latency win or a drop-in full-Transformer
replacement. The highest-value follow-up experiments are suite-robust
eligibility policies that recover a net five-suite selective win,
fused-kernel hardware-counter validation, and multi-layer budget
allocation.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We state the boundary of the present evidence explicitly. First, the systems
measurements use an \emph{unfused} associative factor-matmul path; the
no-write-back descriptor lifecycle is a specified runtime contract, not yet a
measured off-chip behavior, because the available profiler exposed CPU
activities only and observed no fused-like operator. Second, the validated
systems claim is scoped to training throughput, trainable state, and
distributed communication; the prototype is not faster on the unfused forward
path, so no decode-latency claim is made. Third, the language-model quality
evidence is anchored on a single calibrated FFN layer (layer 16) of one
checkpoint family (Qwen2.5-1.5B); multi-layer and multi-model generalization is
future work. Fourth, always-patched replacement is broadly safe only for the
calibrated layer-16 checkpoint and only in aggregate over the five-suite
manifest, with Wikitext-103 and HellaSwag individually regressing; the robust
positive result is the \emph{selective}, budgeted router regime at small actual
patch fractions, and the 2026-07-08 tighter-eligibility Pareto sweep
(Table~\ref{tab:tighterpareto}) establishes that such a net five-suite win is
recoverable at $\Delta_{\max}=-0.20$ under a policy-invariant threshold.
Fifth, the broad held-out manifest currently covers math
reasoning, code generation, and instruction following plus two added general/
commonsense suites, but does not yet cover dialogue or long-context families,
so we do not make a broad-family deployment claim. We report all of these as
boundary evidence rather than omitting the negative controls.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every reported number is tied to a named artifact. The systems sweeps are kept
under explicit run identifiers
(\texttt{ASI3\_reliability\_scaling\_20260518T072507Z}, the June 2026 ASI3
all-eight-NPU sweep, and the nine-run ASI2 reliability set), each recording
platform metadata, device ids, and per-launch JSON. Quality and routing runs
use explicit train/eval text paths, a \texttt{--require-texts} guard that rejects
\texttt{DEFAULT\_TEXTS} fallback, manifest-driven corpora with minimum-size and
overlap checks, and seeded train/eval partitions. The benchmark, distillation,
calibration, router, and broad-validation scripts, the held-out manifest shapes,
and the pulled result summaries are mirrored to a project artifact store. We
report descriptor traffic as a model-side analytic estimate derived from the
measured FFN geometry and clearly separate it from measured latency and
all-reduce numbers in the evidence map (Table~\ref{tab:evidencemap}).

\section{Conclusion}

This paper argues that Transformer FFN weights can be treated not only as
stored parameters, but as runtime-generated operands with explicit lifetimes.
\textsc{MWG-EW} realizes this idea with conditional rank-$r$ descriptors, an
associative execution path, and a proposed no-write-back lifecycle. Full
generated calls reduce estimated descriptor traffic, trainable FFN state, and
distributed gradient communication in an eight-rank 8B-scale FFN systems
benchmark.

The checkpoint-backed Qwen2.5-1.5B experiments identify a concrete
quality-preserving regime: layer 16 is the best current single-layer candidate,
LM-CE calibration improves it relative to persistent low-rank control, and a
benefit-supervised dense-fallback router preserves or improves broad perplexity
at small actual patch-token fractions across leakage-clean, leave-suite-out, and
corrected five-suite validation. The 2026-06-24 three-seed leakage-clean
replication bounds this regime honestly: the same checkpoint is strongly
positive in-distribution and on GSM8K, but the always-patched five-suite
aggregate is above dense, so a uniformly safe always-patched policy is not yet
established. The defensible contribution is therefore the \emph{selective},
budgeted regime at small actual patch fractions, not blanket layer replacement.
The broader contribution is the
parameter-lifetime view itself. The paper therefore establishes a reproducible
path for co-designing memory movement, synchronization, and model quality rather
than optimizing them in isolation.

The new ASI2 reliability replication and fresh ASI3 three-repeat all-NPU sweep strengthen
that path operationally: the same rank family scales repeatably across one,
two, four, and eight-device settings, and the widest ASI3 rank sweep shows a
stable 1.5x--1.65x training-side improvement across a broad rank range. It still
does not license stronger decode claims. The highest-value follow-up
experiments are a fused no-write-back kernel with hardware-counter confirmation
of off-chip descriptor traffic, and multi-layer budget allocation across the
FFN stack. The 2026-07-08 tighter-eligibility Pareto sweep has already closed
the suite-robust eligibility gap: a policy-invariant net five-suite selective
win of $0.9923\times$ at $\sim$2\% actual patching is now established under
three seeds. Each remaining target addresses a specific boundary stated in the evidence
map, and each is required before the parameter-lifetime view can be turned
into a deployable full-Transformer execution primitive.
